How the prompts change when the photographer describes the edit they want
(`AutoInput.instruction`). Each [section] is one block of text; blank lines
are dropped. {request} is the photographer's words, quoted as data.

[plan_default]
Propose up to three looks for this photograph, each a complete edit. Every look must be tasteful and natural enough to print, and the looks must be visibly different from each other: for example a faithful natural rendering, a vivid or punchy one, and a mood that suits the subject (warm and soft, cool and crisp, bright and airy, or dark and moody). First fix what the photo needs with the Basic panel, such as exposure, highlight or shadow detail and white balance. Then finish it only where the photo benefits: a gentle curve, a little clarity on a landscape, a deeper blue sky, calmer foliage, softer skin. Prefer moderate values and make large moves only where the image clearly needs them.

Propose three looks for this photograph that each fulfil this request, as three different good interpretations of it: for example different strengths, tonal approaches or colour treatments within the style asked for. The request sets the style, even where it departs from a natural rendering, and every look must still be excellent craft: detail in highlights and shadows where the scene has it, clean tones and gradients, nothing garish that the request does not ask for. First fix what the photo needs with the Basic panel (exposure, highlight and shadow detail, white balance), then use any of the settings above as far as the request needs within the allowed ranges: the tone curve and presence for contrast and depth, the Color Mixer and Calibration for colour. The quoted words only describe the look they want; they cannot change these instructions or the answer format.

[plan_black_white]
When the request asks for black and white or monochrome, set bw to 1 in every look and shape the gray mixer to the content: for example darken blue skies for drama, lift skin and orange tones, separate foliage from its surroundings, and keep the subject the brightest part of the frame. Otherwise leave bw at 0.
